## Context

Four rows of the operations gap map stayed below `BUILT` in the 2026-10-14 audit for the same reason: a screen exists, an endpoint exists, and a decision that nobody had taken stood between them.

**The phone-order address (rows `1.3`, `1.3b`, `1.3g`).** The New order screen is built for `LOCATION_STAFF` and `LOCATION_MANAGER` (ADR 0039, `orders.md` 5.3). A `DELIVERY` order names a saved address of the resolved customer (`DestinationRequest.customerAddressId`), and `CartService#setDestination` says why in its own Javadoc: an address typed onto a cart would be a second home for a customer's address, outside ADR 0029 erasure and ADR 0015 geocoding, so "the path for an operator taking an order by phone from a customer with no saved address is to save one first". The routes that list, add and move a saved address are `GET`/`POST .../tenants/{tenantId}/customers/{accountId}/addresses` and `PUT .../addresses/{addressId}` on `CustomerController`, declared at `TENANT` scope, gated on `CUSTOMER_PII_REVEAL` (the list) and `CUSTOMER_MANAGE` (the writes). `LOCATION_STAFF` holds neither capability, on purpose: `PlatformRole`'s own comment keeps both off that bundle "so a phone-order operator sees a masked lookup card and never a raw contact value", and `CUSTOMER_MANAGE` would also hand over blacklisting, merging and profile edits on every other customer. The gap map's note that "only `LOCATION_MANAGER` and above" can use the pane is wrong, and the correction is part of the record: the routes are `TENANT`-scoped, "a grant at `LOCATION` scope never covers a `TENANT` route" (`ResourceScope#covers`, `CapabilityEnforcementInterceptor`), and `LOCATION_MANAGER` holds `CUSTOMER_PII_REVEAL` but not `CUSTOMER_MANAGE`, so it cannot add an address either. Only `TENANT_OWNER`, `TENANT_ADMIN` and `SUPPORT_AGENT`, the three roles that hold `CUSTOMER_MANAGE`, could use the pane batch 19 built. Row `1.3a` solved the same shape for create-on-miss with a narrow capability (`CUSTOMER_CREATE`) and a second, location-scoped controller (`OperationsCustomerController`) over the identical write. A manual aggregator entry (row `1.3g`) that is a `DELIVERY` entry reuses the same `DestinationRequest`, so it is blocked by the same thing, and the endpoint has never been called over HTTP by a test.

**Order policy, cards 2 to 5 (row `10.3b`).** The audit of 2026-10-07 found that ten of the thirteen scalar fields on Cards 2 to 5 of the order-policy page are registered, validated, inherited and traced and read by nothing, and the page said so for only some of them. ADR 0150 gave one of the ten a job (`ordering.late_order_threshold_minutes`) and left the rest to "a record that names a reader or removes them". Searching `src/main` on 2026-10-14 finds no reader for any of: `ordering.business_day_start_hour`, `ordering.average_order_minutes`, `ordering.maximum_order_minutes`, `ordering.vat_rate_percent`, `ordering.routing_poll_interval_minutes`, and each has a neighbour that already owns the behaviour (the reporting calendar `reporting.business_day_policies`, row `10.10b`; the preparation bands, ADR 0036; `pricing.tax_profiles` with `valid_from`/`valid_until`, row `4.8a`; the dispatch rules and the sourcing scheduler's deploy property, ADR 0142). `maximum_order_minutes` has no neighbour and would be a fourth lateness severity that `orders.md` 2.7 does not have. Two more, `ordering.auto_accept_eligible_channels` and `ordering.auto_accept_min_prior_orders`, were specified as properties of the acceptance policy document and registered as scalars; `OrderAcceptancePolicy`'s constructor refuses an approval channel under `AUTO_CONFIRM`, so the document cannot say who confirms an order a gate holds back, and no accepted record covers the gap. `settings.md` Card 4 also calls «Кто принимает первым» (courier-first or branch-first acceptance) design-forcing, and row `10.3a` records it as not expressible in the state machine. Three keys that do carry a sentence the screen does not (`ordering.preorder_branch_resolution`, `ordering.operator_promo_code_allowed`, and the minimum prior orders) read as settings that work. `ordering.operator_promo_code_allowed` is registered with the default `false`, while operators apply promo codes today whatever it says (`CartCheckoutAndOrderTests#anOperatorPlacedOrderAppliesAPromoCode`, row `1.3e`), so a reader that honoured the registered default would lock every tenant out.

## Decision

1. **A narrow capability, `customer.address.capture`, lets a phone-order operator give a delivery address without any customer-PII capability.** It grants exactly three operations on the customer the operator has resolved, through routes that are `LOCATION`-scoped beside the location-scoped customer create: (a) a masked list of that customer's saved addresses (label, street without house or flat, city, district, coordinate source, the point, version; never the encrypted fields, the delivery instructions, the entrance, floor, flat or landmark); (b) add one address, with its pin; (c) set the pin of one saved address. It is held at `LOCATION` scope by `LOCATION_STAFF` and `LOCATION_MANAGER`, and, because every role that holds `CUSTOMER_MANAGE` must also hold the narrower code (the rule `CUSTOMER_CREATE` already follows), by `TENANT_OWNER`, `TENANT_ADMIN` and `SUPPORT_AGENT`, so nothing narrows for them. It does not reveal the raw address, edit an address's text, archive one or touch any other customer data. The tenant routes stay as they are for the Customers screen. This amends ADR 0039 (its capability split for the phone-order persona) and ADR 0145 (the pin reaches the persona it was built for) by dated status additions.
2. **Retire the five Card 2 scalars nothing reads.** `ordering.business_day_start_hour`, `ordering.average_order_minutes`, `ordering.maximum_order_minutes`, `ordering.vat_rate_percent` and `ordering.routing_poll_interval_minutes` stop being declared, in both declarations (`OrderingConfigurationKeys` and the tenancy registry `ConfigurationKeys`), their fields leave the order-policy page, their strings leave the three locale catalogues, and one append-only migration deletes any stored `tenant.configuration_values` row for them, because `ConfigurationKeyStartupValidator` refuses to boot over a row whose key has no declaration (the precedent is V0194). Their neighbours already own the behaviour; the page links to them instead (Decision 5).
3. **Both auto-accept gates are read at checkout, and an order a gate holds back follows the acceptance document's own approval path.** `ordering.auto_accept_eligible_channels` (`ALL`, or a comma-separated list of `tenant.sales_channels.code`) and `ordering.auto_accept_min_prior_orders` stay scalars (authored, inherited and traced on Card 3 as they already are), read by `CheckoutOrderWriter` at the order's location. Under `AUTO_CONFIRM`, an order whose channel is not in the list, or whose customer has fewer prior successful orders than the minimum, is not auto-confirmed: it takes the document's own `approvalChannel`, `approvalTimeoutSeconds` and `timeoutAction`, is written `AWAITING_APPROVAL` with `acceptance_mode_snapshot = RESTAURANT_APPROVAL` (the order's own effective mode; the pinned policy is unchanged), and records why in its transition history (`reason_code` `AUTO_ACCEPT_HELD_CHANNEL` or `AUTO_ACCEPT_HELD_PRIOR_ORDERS`). "Prior successful orders" are `COMPLETED` orders of the same customer account in the same brand; a guest counts as zero. Empty gates (`ALL`, `0`) change nothing. The document's invariant relaxes from "an auto-confirm policy has no approval channel" to "an auto-confirm policy may carry an approval path, and when it carries one its timeout is 30 seconds to 30 minutes, as for restaurant approval". When a gate holds an order and the document carries no approval channel, the platform does not strand the order: it auto-confirms, counts it (`horecaos_order_auto_accept_gate_unenforceable_total`), and the page says on the gate fields that they need an approval channel on Card 1. The gates read at checkout, so they apply to every order that goes through `CheckoutService` (storefront, bot, operator, QR, kiosk); a marketplace or aggregator order is written by `JdbcMarketplaceOrderIntake` or `JdbcAggregatorOrderStore` already accepted by the aggregator (ADR 0040) and neither gate applies to it. The same `OrderStateService#paymentCaptured` that resumes a paid order reads the order's own mode snapshot instead of the pinned policy's, so a held order stays held after its payment lands. This amends ADR 0002 by a dated status addition.
4. **Decline «Кто принимает первым»: orders are branch-first.** The order is accepted by the branch that will cook it (`AUTO_CONFIRM`, or `RESTAURANT_APPROVAL` at that branch); a courier does not accept an order before the branch. ADR 0019 said to carry the parameter or refuse it, and this record refuses it. The clause is struck from `settings.md` Card 4 and from the information architecture's 10.3, as ADR 0165 struck the deposit. Revisit when a signed tenant needs courier-first dispatch.
5. **The readers that were waiting on no decision are built in the same wave, at the values that keep today's behaviour.**
   - **Operator promo code.** `ordering.operator_promo_code_allowed` is read where an operator-keyed order is priced (`OperatorOrderingService#fillAndPrice`, which `place` and the quote share): a promo code on an operator-keyed order is refused with `OPERATOR_PROMO_CODE_NOT_ALLOWED` when the resolved value is `false`. The registered default flips from `false` to `true` in both declarations, because the key had no reader and operators apply codes today; a tenant that wants the lock sets `false`, and a tenant that had already stored `false` gets the lock it asked for.
   - **Pre-order branch selection.** The requested time (`requestedFor`) enters the operator branch resolver: candidates are evaluated for service and zone validity at that instant, and when no candidate is open then, `ordering.preorder_branch_resolution` ranks the closed ones: `BY_OPENING_TIME` puts the one that opens soonest first, `BY_DISTANCE` the one nearest the delivery point, and a branch whose own policy takes no pre-order into a closed slot sorts last in either. A `PICKUP` order has no customer point, so `BY_DISTANCE` there falls back to `BY_OPENING_TIME`, and the field says so. The key is read at brand scope, because the question is asked of the brand's branches and the screen and the server's own re-derivation in `OperatorOrderingService#place` must agree on it; the field says a value set on one branch is not read.
   - **Card 5 toggles.** Two new keys, `ordering.operator_callback_flag_allowed` and `ordering.operator_change_due_allowed`, default `true`. When the callback flag is allowed the operator can raise «Перезвонить клиенту» at creation (today it is raised only by the `SET_CALLBACK_REQUESTED` amendment after the order exists); when change-due is not allowed, an operator order with `cashTenderedMinor` is refused and the screen hides «Сдача».
   - **Echoes.** Settings that live on another screen but that the information architecture lists on 10.3 are shown read-only with a link, in a card at the foot of the order-policy page: delivery distance mode (row `3.7`, the standard tariff's mode), the branch order limit (row `10.2c`, read for the branch the scope bar is on, because there is no batched read of it), the kitchen's paid-only hold (ADR 0168), what happens to an address outside every zone and whether a courier sees an order the kitchen is still cooking (both row `10.13`, the courier policy), and the call-centre channel's payment methods and order types (row `10.4b`). The out-of-zone row carries the platform's own caveat, «Пока не применяется»: `delivery.out_of_zone_policy` is registered and editable and the delivery fee resolver does not read it yet (`DeliveryConfigurationKeys`), so an echo that printed the value without saying so would make it read as a setting that works. That is a finding for row `10.13`, recorded here and not fixed here.

## Alternatives considered

| Option | Why not chosen | Revisit when |
|---|---|---|
| Grant `LOCATION_STAFF` `CUSTOMER_PII_REVEAL` and `CUSTOMER_MANAGE` | Reverses the stated intent of ADR 0039 and `PlatformRole` («a masked lookup card and never a raw contact value»); `CUSTOMER_MANAGE` also hands over blacklisting, merging and profile edits on every customer, and the tenant routes would still not be reachable at `LOCATION` scope | The call centre becomes a tenant-level job, held by people with a tenant grant |
| Widen the tenant address routes to accept a `LOCATION` scope | Misdescribes an endpoint the Customers screen and tenant administrators reach tenant-wide; `OperationsCustomerController`'s Javadoc gives the same reason for create-on-miss | Never for the same reason; a second narrow surface is the established shape |
| Let the order carry a free-typed delivery address and no saved one | A second home for a customer's address, outside ADR 0029 erasure and ADR 0015 geocoding (`CartService#setDestination`) | Saved addresses stop being the unit of erasure |
| Mask the point as well as the text | The point is stored in clear on purpose (ADR 0015, ADR 0029: a courier cannot be routed to ciphertext), and the screen needs it for the zone fee, the branch resolver and the map; a rounded point would misprice a zone edge | A zone model that resolves server-side from the address id, so the browser never holds a point |
| Keep the five Card 2 scalars with a «not applied yet» hint (ADR 0150's interim) | A permanent hint is a control that changes nothing; the neighbours own the behaviour | A record names a reader for one (for example a fourth lateness severity in `orders.md` 2.7) |
| Give each retired scalar a reader that reads it (business day from the key, VAT from the key) | A second authority beside the reporting calendar and the dated tax profiles; two answers to "which business day" and "which VAT" is the failure those rows were built to end | Never; the neighbour wins |
| Put the auto-accept gates on the acceptance document (the original spec) | The scalars are already authored, inherited, traced and validated through Card 3 and the settings API, while the document is a versioned policy pinned to each order; moving them re-authors a working surface | The document gains a per-channel structure for another reason |
| Reject an order a gate holds back | A customer who is merely new would be told no; the point of the gate is a person looks at the order | A tenant asks for a hard refusal of unknown channels |
| Apply the gates to aggregator and marketplace orders | Those orders arrive already accepted by the aggregator and bypass checkout; there is nobody to hold them for | A manual-acceptance mode for aggregator orders (ADR 0040 follow-up) |
| Build courier-first acceptance | Reorders the lifecycle in ADR 0019 and has no signed tenant behind it | A tenant whose dispatch model needs a courier to accept before the branch |
| Honour the registered default `false` for the operator promo code | Locks every tenant out of a thing operators do today | Never; a resolved default is never read as a choice (ADR 0150 decision 2) |

## Consequences

### Positive

- A call-centre operator can take a delivery order from a caller the system has never seen, end to end, from the screen's own persona: look the caller up, open the account on a miss, add an address with a pin, quote and place the order. Before this record nobody below `TENANT_ADMIN` could.
- The platform keeps one place where a customer's address lives and one erasure path for it, and the operator still never sees a raw contact value or a house number.
- Every control on Cards 2 to 5 either changes something or is gone; the dead fields stop reading as settings that work, and each retired setting points at the screen that owns it.
- The gates the Delever comparison named (channel eligibility and the new-customer gate) exist, and the acceptance document finally says who confirms a held-back order.

### Negative

- The point of a saved address is returned to a role that cannot read its text. A building location plus a street name is close to an address, so the mask protects the house number and flat, not the neighbourhood. The audit trail (a `SECURITY` fact per list) and the rate limit are the compensating controls.
- A third address surface exists (`OperationsCustomerController` beside `CustomerController` and the storefront's), and the masked street is a heuristic: it drops every token that contains a digit, so a street whose name contains a digit loses part of its name.
- Five keys leave the registry. A tenant that had stored one loses the stored value in the migration; the page never showed that these were not applied, so no behaviour changes, but the value is gone.
- Flipping the default of `ordering.operator_promo_code_allowed` to `true` is a decision about tenants that never opened the page: they keep the behaviour they have, and a tenant that wants the old registered intent has to set `false`.
- An order held back by a gate waits for a person. A tenant that sets a gate and leaves the approval channel at none gets the gate silently unenforced (counted, and surfaced on the page), not a stranded order.
- `OrderAcceptancePolicy` now allows two shapes of `AUTO_CONFIRM`; any reader that assumed the mode alone says whether an approval path exists has to read the channel (there are two such readers, both changed here).

### Accepted trade-offs

- The pre-order ranking is a heuristic over what the platform knows: distance is straight-line from the branch's own coordinates, not a drive time, and a branch with no coordinates is ranked last.
- «Prior successful orders» counts only `COMPLETED` orders of the account in the brand; a customer's history at a sister brand does not count.
- The echoes are links and read-only summaries; they do not edit the setting they show.

## Specification

**Capability and roles.** `Capability.CUSTOMER_ADDRESS_CAPTURE("customer.address.capture", "customer", "address-capture")`, in `PlatformRole` for `TENANT_OWNER`, `TENANT_ADMIN`, `SUPPORT_AGENT`, `LOCATION_MANAGER`, `LOCATION_STAFF` and no other. `PlatformRoleTests` asserts the holders exactly and that every holder of `CUSTOMER_MANAGE` also holds it. The role registry is synchronised from `PlatformRole` at startup (`RoleRegistrySynchronizer`); the activity log's action-code dictionary (`activity-log-action-sentences.ts`) names the three new audit codes in ru, uz-Latn and en, and `activity-log-action-codes-coverage.spec.ts` enforces it.

**Routes** (`OperationsCustomerController`, beside the location-scoped create, all `scope = LOCATION`):

| Route | Capability | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `GET .../locations/{locationId}/customers/{accountId}/addresses` | `customer.address.capture` | the masked list; a `SECURITY` audit fact `customer.address.listed_masked` with the count only; strict rate limit |
| `POST .../customers/{accountId}/addresses` | `customer.address.capture`, `mutating` (Idempotency-Key) | add one address; `coordinateSource` is `OPERATOR_PIN` with a point, or `NOT_GEOCODED`/`LANDMARK_ONLY` without one; `GEOCODER`, `CUSTOMER_PIN` and `LEGACY_UNSOURCED` are refused (ADR 0145 decision 5); audit `customer.address.captured` via `ChangeDocuments.created` with the source and whether it has a pin, never the text or the label |
| `PUT .../customers/{accountId}/addresses/{addressId}/pin` | `customer.address.capture`, `mutating`, `If-Match` | set only the point and the source (`OPERATOR_PIN`) of one saved address; audit `customer.address.pin_set` via `ChangeDocuments.diff` of the source and the has-pin flag and the version |

The account is a predicate of every statement: it must belong to the path's tenant, and it must be visible to the path's brand (`identity_partition_brand_id` is null for a tenant-shared account, or equal to the brand); anything else is `404`, the same answer as "no such customer". The masked read decrypts the address document inside the service and returns only the masked projection.

**Configuration.** Registry changes are made in both declarations and `OrderingConfigurationKeyTests` fails the build if they drift: five removed; `ordering.operator_promo_code_allowed` default `true`; `ordering.operator_callback_flag_allowed` and `ordering.operator_change_due_allowed` added with default `true`. One migration, `V0653__the_five_order_policy_scalars_nothing_read_leave_the_registry.sql`, deletes the stored rows of the five removed keys; `RetiredOrderPolicyKeysMigrationTests` puts the rows back, shows `ConfigurationKeyStartupValidator` refusing to boot over them, runs the very file Flyway ran and shows it satisfied with the live row untouched. The two gates are validated where the one writer applies the rules: `ConfigurationValueRules` holds the shape (`ALL`, or channel codes; a minimum of 0 to 1000) and `AutoAcceptChannelsGuard`, a `ConfigurationWriteGuard`, refuses a code that is not one of the tenant's channels.

**Acceptance.** `AutoAcceptGate` (ordering application) is asked by `CheckoutOrderWriter#create` for the order's channel code and customer account; it reads the two keys at the order's location scope, counts completed orders through `JdbcOrderStore#countCompletedForCustomer`, and answers `Pass`, `Held(reason)` or `Unenforceable`. The held order is written with the document's `approvalChannel` and `timeoutAction` snapshots, the mode snapshot `RESTAURANT_APPROVAL` and an `approval_deadline_at` (`ck_order_approval_deadline` ties the two together), and `CheckoutProgressionStep#awaitApproval` records `AUTO_ACCEPT_HELD_CHANNEL` or `AUTO_ACCEPT_HELD_PRIOR_ORDERS` as the `reason_code` of the `RECEIVED` to `AWAITING_APPROVAL` transition. `OrderStateService#paymentCaptured` reads the order's own mode snapshot, not the pinned policy's. Gate outcomes are counted in `horecaos.order.auto_accept_gate{outcome,reason}`.

**Operator order path.** `OperatorOrderEntryPolicyController` serves `GET .../locations/{locationId}/orders/entry-policy` (`ORDER_PLACE`, `LOCATION`) with the three booleans the New order screen needs; the same values are enforced server-side in `OperatorOrderingService`.

**Branch resolution.** `BranchResolutionQueryService#resolve` takes an optional `requestedFor`, and `POST .../orders/branch-resolution` accepts it; a candidate carries `nextAvailableAt`, `acceptsScheduledOrders` and `distanceMeters`. `BranchResolutionPort.DeliveryBranchMatch` gains the branch's own coordinates (nullable), the existing six-argument constructor stays.

**Console.** `NewOrderApi` calls the location-scoped routes (`capturedAddresses`, `captureAddress`, `placeAddressPin`); `q-address-pin-editor` (a `q-map-pin` with no way to take the pin away) replaces the text editor for a saved address, which the persona cannot read; the page hides what `GET .../orders/entry-policy` says is off and re-asks the branch resolver when the «Позже» time changes. The order-policy page drops the five fields, adds Card 1's hold-back path (the approval fields are shown under auto-confirm, offered with nobody chosen), a notice on Card 3 when the gates have nowhere to send an order, the three Card 5 toggles, and `q-order-policy-neighbours-card` for the echoes.

**Testing.** Each behaviour is named in the implementation checklist with the test that proves it; the both-ends proofs call the production controllers with the JSON the console sends, as `LOCATION_STAFF`, and do not seed what production creates.

## Rollout and rollback

The migration is a delete of rows no code reads; it ships with the code that stops declaring the keys, never before it. Rolling the code back without the rows is safe (the old code declares the keys and the rows are simply absent); rolling forward with a row still present refuses to boot, which is the intended alarm. The capability is additive: rolling back removes the routes and the grant together. The gates default to empty (`ALL`, `0`) so a tenant that never set them sees no change.

## Status addition (2026-10-23)

This addition corrects what the record said the platform did; the Decision, Alternatives and Consequences above are unchanged, and the Implementation status line stays `Built`.

- **Decision 5, pre-order branch selection: the field did not say what the record said it said, and the key accepted a value nothing read.** The record states that the key "is read at brand scope" and that "the field says a value set on one branch is not read". On the day it was written neither half was true of the code: `ordering.preorder_branch_resolution` was declared with no `settableAt`, so `ConfigurationKey` defaulted it to every scope; the order-policy page rendered it like every other field, with its «Override here» action on a branch; and the hint under it explained only the pickup fallback. A manager on the scope bar's branch could write `BY_OPENING_TIME`, see it traced and shown as set there, and `BranchResolutionQueryService#rankForPreOrder` (which reads `ResourceScope.brand(...)`) and the server's re-derivation in `OperatorOrderingService#place` went on using the brand's value. The key is now declared `settableAt(PLATFORM, TENANT, BRAND)` in both declarations (`OrderingConfigurationKeys#PREORDER_BRANCH_RESOLUTION` and the tenancy registry's `ConfigurationKeys#ORDERING_PREORDER_BRANCH_RESOLUTION`; `JdbcConfigurationValueAuthor#set` refuses any other scope with `VALIDATION_FAILED`), the page gives the field its own `settableScopes` so a branch reads «Set at brand level» and offers no write, and the hint says the rule is set for the whole brand. Proved by `OrderingConfigurationKeyTests#thePreOrderBranchRuleIsNotSettableAtABranch` and `order-policy-page.spec.ts` ("does not offer the pre-order branch rule a value of its own on a branch"). The alternative the finding offered, reading the rule at the placement branch with the brand as fallback, was not taken: the resolver ranks the brand's branches against each other, so a per-branch value has no meaning to the question it is asked.
- **Console, the Card 5 toggles: read for the branch the order is placed at, not for the operator's own.** The Specification's Console paragraph says the page "hides what `GET .../orders/entry-policy` says is off", and the server enforces the three toggles at `command.locationId()` -- the branch `OperatorOrderingService#place` and the quote go to, which for a brand-level call-centre operator is the resolver's proposal or her override and not the branch she is signed in at. The screen asked once, in `ngOnInit`, for the operator's own branch, so a pickup order proposed at a branch with promo codes off still showed the promo field and the quote came back `409 OPERATOR_PROMO_CODE_NOT_ALLOWED`, and in the reverse case the field was blanked for a branch that would have taken the code. `NewOrderPage` now asks for the branch the order is placed at (`placeAtLocationId`) and asks again whenever that branch changes (a proposal, an override, a table); an answer for a branch the order has since left is dropped, and a read that fails shows everything and leaves the refusal to the server instead of carrying the previous branch's answer over. Proved by the "the branch the order is placed at decides what the screen offers" specs in `new-order-page.spec.ts`. The finding's other option, carrying the policy on the quote response, was not taken: the quote is only asked once a basket exists, and the screen has to know before the operator types a code.
- **Decision 3, the reason a held order records: it was lost when the order was paid online.** The record says a held order "records why in its transition history" and that `CheckoutProgressionStep#awaitApproval` writes `AUTO_ACCEPT_HELD_CHANNEL` or `AUTO_ACCEPT_HELD_PRIOR_ORDERS`. That is true only of an order that is not paid first. For an order whose payment method captures before confirmation (Click, Payme) `CheckoutService` took the `awaitPayment` branch, which was not given `holdReasonCode`, and `OrderStateService#paymentCaptured` later wrote `PAYMENT_CAPTURED` as the reason of the `PAYMENT_AUTHORIZING` to `AWAITING_APPROVAL` transition: the order sat waiting for a person and nothing in its history said a gate had put it there. `awaitPayment` now takes the reason and keeps it on checkout's own `RECEIVED` to `PAYMENT_AUTHORIZING` transition (the gate decided at checkout; the money may land an hour later), and `paymentCaptured` reads it back from that row, when the order's own mode snapshot is `RESTAURANT_APPROVAL`, and records it as the reason of the move to `AWAITING_APPROVAL`; an order restaurant approval always asks about keeps `PAYMENT_CAPTURED`. No column or migration was added: the checkout transition is the durable note. Proved by `CartCheckoutAndOrderTests` (`aHeldOrderStaysHeldAfterItsPaymentAndExpiresAsTheDocumentSays`, `aHeldOrderPaidOnlineKeepsThePriorOrdersReason`, and `captureSendsARestaurantApprovalOrderToAwaitingApproval` for the unchanged reason).
- **Decision 3, "prior successful orders" is held to the same account and the same brand by a test.** `JdbcOrderStore#countCompletedForCustomer` filters on both, as the record says, but no test would have noticed either predicate going; `CartCheckoutAndOrderTests#priorOrdersAreCountedForTheSameAccountInTheSameBrandOnly` now fails when either is dropped (proved by removing each in turn).
